# ADR 0006 — Split synthesis from voice identity

**Status:** accepted · 2026-08-31
**Supersedes:** the single-component synthesis assumption in ADR 0005.
**Amended:** 2026-09-01 — the step count. See the amendment at the end.

The architecture holds: Kokoro generates, Seed-VC applies the identity. The
step count chosen here (4) did not survive a second listening check and was
raised to 15; the reasoning that produced it is left in place below, because
how it went wrong is the useful part.

The listening check came back: the converted voice was judged good enough to
use, at every step count offered. Since 4, 10 and 25 steps were all acceptable,
**4 is the default** — it is the fastest at 2.40 s end to end, and the step
count stays a config knob (`tts.conversion.diffusion_steps`) so it can be
raised if a larger room or a different reference recording changes the
judgement.

## The mistake this corrects

Chatterbox was chosen on paper in the first week: MIT licence, Turkish and
Italian, zero-shot cloning, watermarking by default. Every box ticked. Its speed
was not measured until weeks later, and it turned out to need **4.3–10.5 s per
sentence** — two to three times the entire latency budget on its own.

Kokoro was in that same first-week survey and was **eliminated in one line**:
"very fast but no voice cloning". That elimination contained an unexamined
assumption — that one component had to both generate speech and carry the
speaker's identity. Nothing required that. Splitting the two makes both easy.

The prompt to re-examine it came from finding an earlier prototype on this
machine that used `RealtimeTTS` with a cloud engine and Applio/RVC for voice
conversion. The architecture was sitting in the user's own home directory while
the plan assumed a single monolithic synthesiser.

The general failure: **measurement inside a chosen architecture cannot detect
that the architecture is wrong.** Every ADR up to 0005 measured carefully and
none questioned the shape.

## Measured

RTX 4070 Laptop. Source is a 9.72 s Italian utterance generated by Kokoro;
target is 7.81 s of the presenter's own recorded voice.

### Generation

| Engine | Longest sentence | RTF | Clones |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chatterbox Multilingual v3 | 10.54 s | 1.29–1.61 | yes |
| **Kokoro-82M** | **0.25 s** | **0.026–0.094** | no |

**42× faster.** Kokoro also does not silence-pad: "Sì." yields 0.97 s of audio
against Chatterbox's 3.00 s.

### Voice conversion — Seed-VC, zero-shot

| `diffusion_steps` | Time for 9.72 s | RTF |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | **1.02 s** | 0.105 |
| 10 | 1.29 s | 0.132 |
| 25 | 1.96 s | 0.201 |

Load 10.1 s from cache, 2875 MiB VRAM, and `convert_voice` exposes
`stream_output`, so the quality/speed trade is a single tunable parameter.

Crucially it is **zero-shot**: no per-voice training, unlike RVC. That keeps the
"clone a volunteer from the audience in fifteen seconds" demonstration possible.

## Budget

For a typical 4.4 s sentence:

```
0.70  commit wait
0.29  recognition        (large-v3-turbo p95)
0.49  translation        (NLLB-600M p95)
0.18  Kokoro
0.57  Seed-VC at 10 steps
-----
2.23  s
```

Against 7.58 s for the Chatterbox path. Inside the 2–3 s target.

VRAM, co-resident: ASR 1223 + MT 929 + Kokoro ~300 + Seed-VC 2875 ≈ 5.3 GB,
which is **less** than the 7.2 GB the Chatterbox pipeline measured.

## Measured end to end, across both processes

Kokoro in `parliamo`, Seed-VC in `seedvc` behind the socket, three sentences,
after warming both stages:

| `diffusion_steps` | Synthesis + conversion | End to end (700 ms commit) | (500 ms commit) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 0.918 s | **2.40 s** | **2.20 s** |
| 10 | 1.111 s | **2.59 s** | 2.39 s |
| 25 | 1.627 s | 3.11 s | 2.91 s |

All inside the 2–3 s target. IPC measured at **8–34 ms**, not the ~1 ms claimed
above before it was measured — small against a second of conversion, but the
earlier figure was a guess and is corrected here.

### A measurement error worth recording

The first end-to-end run reported **3.31 s**, and the difference was entirely
warm-up: the script warmed Kokoro and not the conversion service, so the first
conversion took 2.28 s against the 0.98 s the same sentence took once warm.

Two lessons, both already learned elsewhere in this project and both repeated:

* **Warm every stage, not the one you were thinking about.** The pipeline warms
  ASR and MT at startup for exactly this reason; the conversion service needs
  the same treatment before a rehearsal.
* **Components measured alone are faster than components measured together.**
  Seed-VC alone ran at RTF 0.132; co-resident with Kokoro it runs at 0.16–0.18.
  This is the same error as summing per-model VRAM estimates in ADR 0003, in a
  different currency.

## Quality — settled by listening, not by measurement

Speed was measured; whether the converted audio *sounds like the speaker* is a
listening judgement and nothing else. Samples at 4, 10 and 25 diffusion steps
were handed over, and all three came back acceptable — which is why this ADR is
accepted rather than proposed, and why the default is 4: it is the fastest of
three options that were all judged good enough.

Had it come back the other way, the fallbacks in order were:

1. **More diffusion steps** — 25 costs 1.96 s and still fits.
2. **RVC via Applio**, already installed and already used by the earlier
   prototype. Better fidelity per voice, but needs training per voice, which
   removes the live-volunteer demonstration.
3. **XTTS v2** as a single component at ~600 ms first audio — slower than
   Kokoro but simpler than a two-stage pipeline. Currently broken in the
   `translator` environment (`transformers` removed `isin_mps_friendly`).

## Chatterbox keeps one job

Not deleted. For the **ethics demonstration** its weaknesses do not matter and
one of its features is essential:

* A 10 s wait is fine, even useful, when the wait is the point being shown.
* It watermarks output by default, which is what makes "we cloned it, and here
  is the watermark proving it" possible in one breath.

So: Kokoro plus Seed-VC on the live path, Chatterbox for the cloning
demonstration.

## Deployment shape: two processes, not one

An attempt was made to collapse everything into the `parliamo` environment.
The result splits cleanly:

**Kokoro moves in.** It pins no torch version, installed without disturbing
anything, and is *faster* there than in the older environment — RTF 0.019–0.073
against 0.026–0.094, longest sentence 0.195 s against 0.249 s, because torch
2.11+cu128 is newer. 587 MiB VRAM. Verified afterwards: torch 2.11+cu128 with
CUDA, transformers 5.16.1, ctranslate2 4.8.1 all intact.

**Seed-VC does not.** Its torch and transformers pins turned out to be
conservative — it imports fine against 2.11 and 5.16.1 — but it fails at load
with::

    BigVGAN._from_pretrained() missing 2 required keyword-only
    arguments: 'proxies' and 'resume_download'

That is a genuine `huggingface_hub` API break, not a cautious pin. Forcing an
older hub into `parliamo` would put `datasets` and `transformers` at risk, which
is the trade that broke the environment once already.

So the pipeline runs as **two processes**: the main pipeline in `parliamo`
(capture, VAD, ASR, MT, Kokoro), and voice conversion in `seedvc` behind a local
socket. Conversion costs ~0.57 s of compute for a typical sentence; local IPC
adds about a millisecond, so the split is free in latency terms. It also
permanently isolates the dependency conflict instead of re-litigating it on
every upgrade.

This is the same shape the user's earlier prototype used, which reached Applio
over HTTP on port 6969.

## Environment discipline

Seed-VC pins `torch==2.4.0` and `transformers==4.46.3`. It was installed in an
isolated `seedvc` environment, and `parliamo` was verified afterwards to still
hold torch 2.11+cu128 with CUDA. This follows directly from `chatterbox-tts`
silently downgrading torch to the CPU wheel earlier and breaking every other
stage.

Two Windows-specific obstacles, recorded because anyone repeating this will hit
them: torch 2.4.0 fails to load `fbgemm.dll` (fixed by using 2.6.0), and
`webrtcvad` needs MSVC build tools (fixed with the prebuilt `webrtcvad-wheels`,
installing `resemblyzer` with `--no-deps`).

`SeedVCWrapper(device="cuda")` also fails: the library calls `.type` on the
argument, so it must be given `torch.device("cuda")`.

## Amendment, 2026-09-01 — four diffusion steps was not enough

The cloned voice was used in a live run and judged not good enough. Two causes,
one in the reference and one in this ADR.

### The reference was the wrong file, and the fault was in the advice

The reference suggested for the first live test was a single 7.8 s in-situ
sentence. Measured afterwards, the laptop's Intel Smart Sound driver gates
non-speech to **digital silence**, and across the 40 in-situ recordings it
removes between **16% and 50%** of every file. The one chosen by hand was
**49.5% exact zeros** — the second worst of the forty. Every file also sits
around -30 dBFS.

It shows in the output as well as the input: converted with that reference the
result measured -24.2 dBFS peak 0.499, against -12.3 dBFS with a clean one.
Seed-VC carries the reference's level characteristics through, so a quiet,
holed reference produces quiet, holed speech.

`parliamo/tts/reference.py` and `scripts/build_voice_reference.py` now assemble
a reference by ranking candidates on how little the driver removed, dropping
the dead air inside the ones kept, and normalising. Choosing by hand is how the
worst file got picked.

### Four steps leaves audible noise

With a good reference the listener rejected 4 steps for "digital breaking" —
which is what an under-converged diffusion process sounds like. The residual
noise has not been resolved into speech yet. The original check that accepted
4 steps was made against the degraded reference, where the artefact was masked
by everything else that was wrong.

Measured on a 4.42 s sentence, one variable at a time:

| reference | steps | conversion | est. total | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12 s | 15 | **1.64 s** | 3.03 s | **acceptable — adopted** |
| 28 s | 10 | 1.78 s | 3.17 s | acceptable |
| 12 s | 25 | 2.33 s | 3.72 s | |
| 28 s | 15 | 2.34 s | 3.73 s | |
| 28 s | 25 | 3.29 s | 4.68 s | best quality, outside the budget |

**Steps are the stronger lever.** Going 25 → 15 saves 40%; shortening the
reference 28 s → 12 s saves 29%. Both cost time, which was not previously
known: conversion scales with the *reference* length as well as the source.

### Decision: `diffusion_steps: 15`, with a 12-second reference

The short reference is preferred beyond its cost. A volunteer on stage gives
about fifteen seconds, so a setting that works at 12 s works for the volunteer
demonstration and for the presenter without carrying two configurations.

The total lands near 3.0 s, above the 2.40 s this ADR originally claimed and at
the edge of the 2–3 s target. Lowering `vad.min_silence_ms` from 700 to 500
brings it to ~2.8 s, and that trade has not yet been measured on continuous
speech.

### What this cost, and the general lesson

The 2.40 s figure in the table above was measured with settings that were later
judged unusable. It stood as the project's headline latency for a day.

**A listening judgement is a measurement, and it is only as good as the sample
it was taken on.** The first check accepted every step count because the
reference was bad enough to hide the difference between them. Measuring the
quality of A against a broken B tells you about B.

### Still open

* **A reference recorded on better hardware.** The ceiling here is the laptop
  microphone. Thirty seconds read into a phone in a quiet room would have no
  gating and full level, and may well make fewer steps sufficient — quality and
  speed from the same change. Deferred, with a recording to come.
* **Whether the live path should carry the cloned voice at all.** Without
  conversion the pipeline runs at ~1.4 s. The talk's argument rests on the
  cloning *demonstration*, where several seconds of latency is acceptable and
  arguably part of the point. Keeping the cloned voice for the whole hour is a
  preference, not a requirement, and it costs 1.6 s on every sentence.
